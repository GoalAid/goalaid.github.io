## Quick answer: the cheapest way to rent a car

1. **Compare first.** Search a comparison site to see several companies side by side for your dates.
2. **Book early, but choose free cancellation.** About 3–6 weeks ahead is often cheapest. Re-check the price closer to your trip.
3. **Check off-airport locations.** They often skip airport concession fees.
4. **Use the coverage you already have.** Your auto policy or credit card may make the counter's damage waiver unnecessary.
5. **Return the car full of gas, on time, and take photos.**

## What you need to rent a car in the US

| Requirement                     | What to know                                                                                                                                                                                           |
| ------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Driver's license**            | A valid, full license (not a learner's permit) in the renter's name. International visitors can usually use their home license. Bring an International Driving Permit (IDP) if yours isn't in English. |
| **Minimum age**                 | Usually **21** (a few companies allow 20). Drivers **under 25** pay a young-renter fee. New York and Michigan require companies to rent to drivers **18+**.                                            |
| **Payment card**                | A **credit card** in the driver's name is the easiest option. Debit cards are accepted at many locations but often come with extra checks (see below).                                                 |
| **Deposit / hold**              | Expect a temporary hold on your card, often a few hundred dollars on top of the rental cost. It's released after you return the car.                                                                   |
| **ID and booking confirmation** | International renters need a passport. Keep your voucher or confirmation number on your phone.                                                                                                         |

### Can I rent a car with a debit card?

Often yes, but read the location's policy first. Companies that accept debit cards may:

* require proof of a return flight (at airports),
* run a soft credit check,
* place a larger hold on your account,
* limit you to certain car classes (no luxury cars or large SUVs).

Comparison sites let you filter or check each supplier's payment policy before you pay.

### Renting a car under 25

Most major companies rent to drivers 21–24 but add a **young-renter surcharge**, often about $19–$35 per day depending on the company and location. Ways to lower it:

* **AAA membership:** Hertz has historically waived the young-renter fee for AAA members aged 20–24.
* **USAA members** (military families) often get the fee waived at participating companies.
* **Business rentals** booked under an employer's corporate account often remove the fee.
* Enter your **real age when searching**. Good comparison tools include the fee in the total, so you can see the true cheapest option.

## Rental car insurance: what you actually need

At the counter you'll usually be offered four products. Here is what each one does and whether you might already have it.

| Coverage offered at the counter              | What it covers                                                | You may already have it through…                                                                     |
| -------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Collision / Loss Damage Waiver (CDW/LDW)** | Damage to or theft of the rental car. Often **$30–$40+/day**. | Your personal auto policy (if you carry collision and comprehensive) or a credit card rental benefit |
| **Supplemental Liability (SLI)**             | Damage or injury you cause to others, above the state minimum | Your personal auto liability coverage                                                                |
| **Personal Accident Insurance (PAI)**        | Medical costs for you and your passengers                     | Health insurance                                                                                     |
| **Personal Effects Coverage**                | Belongings stolen from the car                                | Homeowners or renters insurance                                                                      |

**Before you travel, make two calls:**

1. **Your auto insurer.** Ask: *Does my policy cover rental cars, including collision, liability and "loss of use" fees? What's my deductible?*
2. **Your credit card issuer.** Ask: *Is rental coverage* ***primary**** or* ***secondary****? Which vehicles and countries are excluded?* Primary coverage pays first, so you don't have to file a claim on your own policy.

To use a credit card's coverage, you typically must **pay for the whole rental with that card** and **decline the company's CDW/LDW**.

**When buying at the counter makes sense:** you don't own a car (no personal auto policy), your card has no rental benefit, you're renting a vehicle class your card excludes (trucks, exotic cars, large vans), or you want zero hassle if something goes wrong.

## Common car rental fees (and how to avoid them)

| Fee                                    | Typical trigger                      | How to avoid or reduce it                                                                                                                                                        |
| -------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Airport concession / facility fees** | Picking up at an airport counter     | Compare the price at an off-airport or neighborhood branch                                                                                                                       |
| **Young-renter fee**                   | Driver under 25                      | AAA/USAA waivers, corporate rates, compare suppliers                                                                                                                             |
| **Additional driver fee**              | Adding a second driver               | Spouses and domestic partners are free at many companies and in some states (e.g., California). Loyalty programs and Costco Travel bookings often include one free extra driver. |
| **One-way / drop fee**                 | Returning to a different location    | Compare routes. Some Florida and Southwest one-way routes are cheap in seasonal "drive-out" periods.                                                                             |
| **Fuel charges**                       | Returning the tank less than full    | Refill within ~10 miles of the return and keep the receipt. Prepaid fuel only pays off if you return the car nearly empty.                                                       |
| **Toll programs**                      | Using the company's toll transponder | Bring your own E-ZPass/SunPass if it's compatible, pay tolls online, or set your map app to avoid tolls                                                                          |
| **Late return**                        | Returning after the grace period     | Most companies charge an extra hour or a full extra day. Know your return time.                                                                                                  |
| **Cleaning / smoking fees**            | Sand, pet hair, smoke                | Shake out the mats and remove trash before returning                                                                                                                             |
| **Upgrades and add-ons**               | GPS, satellite radio, car seats      | Use your phone for navigation. Airlines check car seats for free.                                                                                                                |

**Tip:** Always compare the **total price including taxes and fees**, not the daily base rate. A $29/day headline rate can become $55/day at the counter.

## 10 ways to get a cheaper car rental

1. **Compare several companies at once.** Prices for the same car class at the same airport can differ a lot between companies. A comparison site like **[DiscoverCars]([AFFILIATE)** shows them side by side with mandatory fees included.
2. **Book with free cancellation, then re-check.** Rental prices fall as well as rise. If you find a lower price later, re-book and cancel the original.
3. **Book about 3–6 weeks out**, and earlier for holidays, spring break, and summer in Florida, Hawaii and national-park gateways.
4. **Check off-airport branches.** A short rideshare to a city location can save airport fees. Do the math, including the rideshare fare.
5. **Try weekly rates.** A 5-day rental can cost more than 7 days, because weekly rates often kick in at 5+ days.
6. **Pick the smallest car you'll be comfortable in.** Economy and compact cars are cheapest. Free upgrades are common when small cars run out.
7. **Use memberships and warehouse clubs.** Costco Travel, AAA, AARP and some airline or hotel programs offer discounts or free extras.
8. **Join free loyalty programs.** Enterprise Plus, Hertz Gold Plus Rewards, Avis Preferred and National Emerald Club let you skip the counter, and some let you choose your own car.
9. **Decline coverage you don't need.** See the [insurance section](#rental-car-insurance-what-you-actually-need). This is often the biggest single saving.
10. **Consider peer-to-peer (Turo) for unusual needs.** It can be cheaper for specific models or in areas with few rental branches. Check insurance and mileage limits carefully.

## How to rent a car, step by step

1. **Choose your pickup location, dates and times.** Include your real age so young-driver fees are shown.
2. **Compare offers by total price**, supplier rating, fuel policy (look for "full to full"), mileage (look for "unlimited") and cancellation terms.
3. **Book and save your voucher.** Join the supplier's free loyalty program to skip the counter if you can.
4. **At pickup:** bring your license, the card you booked with, and ID. Decline extras you don't need. Read the total on the contract before signing.
5. **Inspect the car.** Walk around it and **record a video** of every panel, the wheels, the windshield, the interior, the fuel level and the mileage. Ask staff to note any damage.
6. **During the trip:** know the toll setup and keep the roadside-assistance number handy.
7. **Return:** refuel nearby, remove your belongings, take photos of the car and the dashboard, and get a closed-out receipt before you leave.

### Pickup and return checklist

* ☐ License, credit card (in the driver's name), ID/passport
* ☐ Booking confirmation
* ☐ Proof of insurance or credit-card benefit details
* ☐ Walk-around video at pickup
* ☐ Fuel receipt at return
* ☐ Return photos and final receipt

## Frequently asked questions

### What do I need to rent a car in the US?

A valid driver's license, a credit card (or an accepted debit card) in the driver's name, and your booking confirmation. International visitors also need a passport, and an International Driving Permit if their license isn't in English.

### How old do you have to be to rent a car?

Usually 21, with a young-renter fee for drivers under 25. Some companies allow 20-year-olds. In New York and Michigan, companies must rent to drivers 18 and older, though fees are higher.

### Can I rent a car without a credit card?

Yes, at many locations, using a debit card. Expect extra requirements such as a return-flight itinerary, a credit check, a larger hold, or limits on car class. Check the supplier's payment policy before booking.

### Do I need to buy the rental company's insurance?

Not always. Your personal auto policy and many credit cards cover rental cars. Call both before your trip. Buy the counter coverage if you have no other protection or want the simplest claims process.

### How far in advance should I book a rental car?

For most US trips, about 3–6 weeks ahead gets good prices. Book earlier for holidays and peak summer. Choose free cancellation so you can re-book if prices drop.

### Is it cheaper to rent a car at the airport?

Usually not. Airport locations add concession and facility fees. Off-airport branches are often cheaper, but compare the savings against the cost of getting there.

### Can I add another driver?

Yes. Every driver must be present with a valid license at pickup. Many companies charge a daily fee, but spouses and partners are often free, and some states and memberships waive the fee.

### What if my plans change?

With a free-cancellation booking, you can usually change or cancel at no cost up to 48 hours before pickup. Check your booking's specific terms.

### Can I take a US rental car to Canada or Mexico?

Canada is usually allowed with major companies. Tell them at booking. Mexico typically requires special permission and Mexican liability insurance, and many companies don't allow it.
